Opposite cube faces have equal and opposite flux in a uniform field.

Why does the net flux vanish?

The area vector of each face points outward. For every face with normal n, its opposite face has normal −n and the same area.

Because the electric field is uniform, each opposite-face pair contributes equal-magnitude flux with opposite sign. The sum over all six faces is zero.

  1. Determine each outward normal vector.
  2. Calculate the dot product E · A for each face.
  3. Add all six signed contributions.
  4. Use Gauss' law: Φnet = qenc/ε0 = 0, because qenc = 0.

Important: Zero net flux does not mean the electric field is zero. It means the signed inward and outward contributions cancel.

Learning goals and model assumptions

After this experiment, you should be able to identify the six outward normals of a cube, calculate signed flux face by face, distinguish zero net flux from zero electric field, and explain the flux cancellation using Gauss' law.

Lecture connection: Chapter 24, Example 24.1, “Flux Through a Cube” (page 7). In its axis-aligned case, the entering face contributes −EL², the leaving face contributes +EL², and the other four contribute 0.

Physical assumptions: Constant spatially uniform electric field E = (E, 0, 0), ideal closed cube with outward normals, no enclosed charge. The cube is displayed at a fixed visual size for clarity; its physical edge length changes the calculated area and flux. The camera motion does not physically rotate the cube.
