How does flux vary with angle?

Graph requires canvas support.

The graph uses the current field strength and area. The vertical axis is signed flux in N·m²/C. The dot follows the angle slider.

What the picture means

At θ = 0°: The field and chosen surface normal point in the same direction. Flux is +EA.

At θ = 90°: The field is parallel to the surface. Flux is 0.

At θ = 180°: The field and chosen normal point in opposite directions. Flux is −EA.

θ is measured from the surface normal, not from the surface itself. Positive flux means field is oriented with the chosen normal; negative flux means opposite.

Lab notes & learning goals

After this laboratory you should be able to define the area vector, calculate the electric flux through a flat surface in a uniform field, interpret the sign of flux, and distinguish the surface-normal angle from the angle to the plane.

Model assumptions: Uniform static electric field along +x; flat rectangular open surface; user-chosen orientation of the normal. The visualization is an idealized geometry. This is not yet Gauss' law for a closed surface (that is addressed in later laboratories).

Suggested lecture check: For E = 620 N/C and a = 0.10 m, b = 0.20 m, compare flux at 0° and 90° with the example on page 4 of Chapter 24.
