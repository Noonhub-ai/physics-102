Calculation — just three steps

    Important: At a charged conducting surface, the electric field jumps from zero just inside to E = |Q|/(4π ε₀ a²) just outside. The simulation reports the outside limit when r = a.

    Discover the physics

    Surprising result: A charged solid conducting sphere and an uncharged-cavity spherical conductor with the same outer radius and total charge produce the same field everywhere outside. Both also have zero field in their interior regions.
    1. Choose Solid sphere and move P inside. Observe E = 0.
    2. Switch to Hollow shell. Move P first into the cavity, then into the metal. Both give E = 0 (with no charge in the cavity).
    3. Move P outside. The field falls as 1/r².
    4. Change the sign of Q. The field reverses but its magnitude stays the same.

    Model assumptions: spherical symmetry, electrostatic equilibrium, isolated conductor, no charge inside the shell cavity, and no external applied field.
