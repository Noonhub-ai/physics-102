Discover the physics

Important: An external charge can change the electric field at individual points on the sphere, but its contribution to the net flux through this closed surface is zero.
  • Move a charge outward: The net flux changes when its position passes the spherical boundary.
  • Increase the sphere radius: If the same charges stay enclosed, net flux stays unchanged.
  • Try +4 µC and −4 µC inside: Their net enclosed charge is zero, even though the field need not be zero.
  • Reverse a charge's sign: The sign of its contribution to flux reverses.

Why a sphere?

Gauss' law is valid for any closed surface. A sphere is especially convenient when calculating the electric field produced by a charge distribution with spherical symmetry. Here it is used to make the meaning of enclosed charge visible.

How to read the arrows

Green arrows show the direction of the local electric field at selected surface points, calculated from all charges (including those outside). They are not a count of flux lines. Their displayed lengths are normalized for legibility, not scaled as a measurement of field magnitude.
