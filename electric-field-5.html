Step-by-step calculation

The equations update whenever you move the test charge or change either charge.

1. Calculate the distance

The source is at the origin, and the test charge is at (x, y). Use the Pythagorean theorem.

2. Calculate the electric field

The source charge Q creates a field at the test point. The field direction is away from positive Q and toward negative Q.

|E| = kₑ |Q| / r²

3. Calculate the force on the test charge

The force follows F⃗ = q₀ E⃗. A positive test charge feels force along the field; a negative test charge feels force opposite the field.

|F| = |q₀| |E|

4. Compare with Coulomb's law

The same force magnitude can be calculated directly using the two charges and their separation.

|F| = kₑ |Q q₀| / r²

Units: kₑ = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²; 1 μC = 10⁻⁶ C; 1 nC = 10⁻⁹ C. For ideal point charges, the electric field is independent of the test charge.
