2 · Understand the physics

E(P) = ∑i ke qi · (rP − ri) / |rP − ri|3
ke = 8.99 × 109 N·m2/C2
Step 1 — Define a test point

The field at P is the force that a small positive test charge would experience per unit charge. Moving the probe changes the observation point, not the source charges.

Step 2 — Determine direction

A positive source creates field vectors pointing away from it. A negative source creates field vectors pointing toward it. These field-line arrows always indicate the direction a positive test charge would initially accelerate.

Step 3 — Calculate each contribution

For a point source, the magnitude is |Eᵢ| = kₑ |qᵢ| / rᵢ². The simulation uses source charge in μC and distances in meters. The values of Eₓ and Eᵧ update as you move P.

Step 4 — Add vector components

With two sources, Eₓ = E₁ₓ + E₂ₓ and Eᵧ = E₁ᵧ + E₂ᵧ. The magnitude is √(Eₓ² + Eᵧ²) and the direction is atan2(Eᵧ, Eₓ). The local arrow shows the resultant field.

Step 5 — Interpret field lines correctly

Field-line density illustrates field strength qualitatively. Lines never cross, because the electric field at a given nonsingular point has only one direction. The displayed line count is proportional to the charge magnitude (4 lines per μC). For unequal opposite charges, surplus lines come from or go to infinity. The edges of the drawing are a window into a larger field, not physical endpoints. These curves are computed from the field; they are not physical particle trajectories.
