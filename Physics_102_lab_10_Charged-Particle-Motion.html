Detailed solution — Example 13

The textbook example uses an electron with vₓ = 3.00 × 10⁶ m/s, E = 200 N/C, and L = 0.100 m. Its figure shows an upward electric field, so the electron accelerates downward. Open each step to see the derivation.

Step 1 — Identify forces and choose coordinates

Let +x be right and +y be up. Inside a uniform electric field, the electric force is F⃗ = q E⃗. An electron has q = −1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, so its electric force points opposite to E⃗. Neglect gravity.

Fᵧ = q Eᵧ ; m aᵧ = q Eᵧ ; aₓ = 0
Step 2 — Calculate acceleration (part A)

The electron mass is 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg. For an upward field, Eᵧ = +200 N/C.

aᵧ = q Eᵧ / m
= [ (−1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹)(+200) ] / (9.109 × 10⁻³¹)
≈ −3.52 × 10¹³ m/s²

The negative sign means downward acceleration.

Step 3 — Find transit time (part B)

There is no horizontal electric force, so vₓ remains constant while in the field. Divide the plate length by the horizontal speed:

t = L/vₓ = 0.100/(3.00 × 10⁶)
= 3.33 × 10⁻⁸ s
Step 4 — Find vertical displacement (part C)

The electron enters with vᵧ,initial = 0. Use the constant-acceleration equation:

Δy = vᵧ,initial t + ½ aᵧ t²
= ½(−3.52 × 10¹³)(3.33 × 10⁻⁸)²
≈ −1.95 × 10⁻² m = −1.95 cm

The electron is 1.95 cm below its entry position at the exit plane, assuming the plates are far enough apart to avoid impact. The simulation uses an 8.0 cm gap, so the textbook default passes through without collision.

Step 5 — Predict the exit velocity and path

Horizontal velocity remains +3.00 × 10⁶ m/s. Vertical velocity grows linearly with time:

vᵧ = aᵧ t ≈ (−3.52 × 10¹³)(3.33 × 10⁻⁸) ≈ −1.17 × 10⁶ m/s

Combining horizontal uniform motion and vertical uniformly accelerated motion gives a parabolic trajectory inside the plates:

y(x) = (qEᵧ / (2 m vₓ²)) x²

This extension follows from the chapter's constant-acceleration equations.
