Derivation and detailed solution

At P, each small charge element contributes a field along the x-axis. For a positive rod the field points to the left (−x), away from the rod; for a negative rod it points right (+x).

|E| = ke |Q| / [a(a + L)]

Valid for a uniformly charged, thin rod and a point P on its axis outside the rod, with a > 0. ke = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C².

1. Define the geometry and linear charge density

Let x measure distance from point P to a small rod element. The rod begins at x = a and ends at x = a + L. For uniform charge:

λ = Q / L   and   dq = λ dx

2. Write the field of a small element dq

For a small element at distance x, Coulomb's law gives a field magnitude:

d|E| = ke |dq| / x²

Because all field contributions at P lie along the same axis, they add algebraically. The field direction is determined by the sign of Q.

3. Integrate along the rod

Substitute |dq| = |λ| dx and integrate from the near end (a) to the far end (a + L):

|E| = ke |λ| ∫aa+L dx/x²
= ke |λ| [−1/x]aa+L
= ke |λ| (1/a − 1/(a+L))

Using |λ| = |Q|/L and simplifying gives |E| = ke|Q|/[a(a+L)].

5. Check the result and understand the limits

If Q = 0, then E = 0. For fixed Q and L, moving P farther away reduces |E|. Far from the rod (a ≫ L), the field approaches that of a point charge: |E| ≈ ke|Q|/a².
