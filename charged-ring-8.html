Signed field component for a uniformly charged thin ring in vacuum, with the ring centered at x = 0. Use ke = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C².

Step 1 — Geometry: every charge element has the same distance r

Take a tiny ring element dq. By the right triangle in the diagram, its distance to observation point P is

r = √(a² + x²)

The ring has uniform linear charge density λ = Q/(2πa). Therefore dq = λ dℓ, where dℓ is a small arc length.

Step 2 — Write the electric field of a charge element

By Coulomb's law, the field magnitude of a small element is

|dE| = ke |dq| / r²

The component along the x-axis is signed: dEx = ke x dq / r³, since cos θ = x/r. This formula automatically gives the correct direction for negative Q or negative x.

Step 3 — Use symmetry to cancel perpendicular components

Opposite ring elements at the same radial distance produce equal and opposite field components perpendicular to the x-axis. These cancel in pairs around the entire ring.

Only axial components add. Because r and x are the same for every ring element:

Ex = ∫ dEx = [ke x / r³] ∫ dq
Step 4 — Integrate over the total ring charge

The integral of dq around the entire ring equals Q. Substitute r² = a² + x²:

Ex = ke Qx / (a²+x²)3/2

The sign gives the direction. For Q > 0, the field points away from the ring's plane on either side; for Q < 0, it points toward the ring's plane.

Step 6 — Check three important special cases

At the center (x = 0): E = 0 by symmetry for any finite ring radius.

Far from the ring (|x| ≫ a): |E| ≈ ke|Q|/x², approaching a point charge.

Near the center (|x| ≪ a): Ex ≈ keQx/a³, linear in signed x.

Changing Q from positive to negative reverses the field direction; setting Q = 0 produces zero field everywhere.
