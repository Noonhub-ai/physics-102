Interactive Laboratory 07 · Electric Potential

Potential Energy of Two Charges

Change the signs of two charges and move them closer or farther apart. Discover why attraction and repulsion correspond to different signs of electric potential energy, and how work changes that energy.

U(r) = kq₁q₂/r   ·   ΔU = Ufinal − Uinitial   ·   Wfield = −ΔU

1. Move the charges

Charge q₁ is fixed at the left. Drag charge q₂ horizontally, or adjust r. The distance r and force arrows change with the experiment. The illustration is to scale for separation; charge markers and arrows are schematic.

2. Experiment controls

Assume ideal point charges in vacuum. U is defined to be zero when r tends to infinity.

3. Live potential energy and force

4. Predict the sign

For your current charges, is the interaction potential energy positive, negative, or zero?

    5. Explore the energy curve U(r)

    The graph updates for your selected charges. The point marks the current separation. Negative values indicate an attractive pair, positive values a repulsive pair; the force also depends on the slope of this graph.

    6. Calculate the work between two separations

    Choose an initial separation ri and final separation rf. The charges stay the same throughout this move.

    For a slow move with no change in kinetic energy, the external work is Wexternal = ΔU. The field work is Wfield = −ΔU. The charges are not assumed to follow this chosen move naturally.
