Interactive Laboratory 04 · Electric Potential

Proton & Electron Accelerator

Two particles begin at rest and accelerate through the same potential difference. Observe how the charge sign determines the direction of acceleration, while particle mass determines the final speed.

ΔK = −qΔV   ·   ½mv² = e|ΔV|   ·   v = √(2e|ΔV|/m)

1. Watch both particles accelerate

The electric field points right in both lanes. A proton starts on the left and accelerates right; an electron starts on the right and accelerates left. The animation uses a slowed-down time scale. For an equal plate separation and voltage, the lighter electron reaches the opposite plate first; the animation illustrates the relative flight times, not their actual nanosecond timing.

Ready — both particles begin at rest.

2. Adjust the accelerating voltage

Both particles gain the same kinetic energy because they have charges of equal magnitude:

ΔK = e|ΔV|

Choose an acceleration voltage to compare their final speeds.

Key discovery: For the same accelerating voltage, both gain equal kinetic energy, but the electron's final speed is about times the proton's. At equal voltage, the mass difference is why the speeds differ. (The bars use the same speed scale.)

4. Make a prediction

What happens to both final speeds if the accelerating voltage is increased from 100 V to 400 V?

Hint: speed is proportional to the square root of the accelerating voltage.
