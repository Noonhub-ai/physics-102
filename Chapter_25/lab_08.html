Interactive Laboratory 08 · Electric Potential

From a Potential Graph to Electric Field

The slope of a graph of electric potential V versus position x tells us both the magnitude and direction of the electric field. Explore a piecewise-linear potential graph and watch the electric-field graph change with its slope.

Ex = −dV/dx   ·   1 V/cm = 100 V/m

1. Explore the electric-potential graph

Drag the purple position marker horizontally, or use the position slider. Choose a region to highlight its slope triangle. The graph follows the three-segment pattern used in Chapter 25 Problem 36.

2. Experiment controls

The potential rises from 0 to H over 0–1 cm, is constant from 1–3 cm, and falls to 0 over 3–4 cm. Corners are included in the x slider.

3. Electric-field graph Eₓ(x)

The three horizontal pieces show the field within each interval. Hollow circles mark the two corner positions, where the idealized graph has no single derivative. The purple marker tracks the selected position.

4. Live result

5. Determine the field in all three regions

For each section, calculate the slope of V(x) and reverse its sign to obtain Eₓ. Remember to convert centimeters to meters.

Physical interpretation: In Region A, potential rises with x, so E points left (negative x). In Region B, potential is constant, so E = 0. In Region C, potential falls with x, so E points right (positive x).
