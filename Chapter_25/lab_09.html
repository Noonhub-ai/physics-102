Interactive Laboratory 09 · Electric Potential

Electric Dipole Potential

Two equal-magnitude, opposite charges form an electric dipole. Move point P around them to see how positive and negative potential contributions combine—and discover why V = 0 does not mean E = 0.

V(P) = kq/r+ − kq/r−   ·   p = 2aq

1. Explore the dipole

Drag P to any position away from the charges. The positive charge is at x = −a, the negative charge at x = +a. Both dashed distances and the local electric-field arrow update with P. Diagram coordinates are to scale; charge markers and field-arrow length are schematic.

2. Adjust the geometry

We prevent P from overlapping either ideal point charge, where the electric potential and field are singular.

3. Live potential and electric field

Signed potential contributions

The bar chart shows scalar potential contributions. The electric-field arrow in the diagram instead comes from adding field vectors.

4. Make a prediction

At any point on the perpendicular bisector (x = 0, away from the charges), what is V?

    5. Three discovery experiments

    Pick an experiment to observe how the sign of V changes.

    Important: The dipole's perpendicular bisector is an equipotential line with V = 0, but at any finite point on it the field is directed from +q toward −q and is nonzero.
