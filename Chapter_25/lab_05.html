Electric Potential of a Point Charge

Move a point P around a source charge Q. Discover why electric potential is a scalar, why its sign follows the source charge, and why electric potential decreases as 1/r while electric-field magnitude decreases as 1/r².

V = kQ/r   ·   Er = kQ/r²   ·   V(∞) = 0

4. Explore the two distance laws

The curves are normalized to their magnitudes at r = 1 m, for any nonzero Q. They show how the magnitudes change with distance, independent of charge sign. The vertical dotted line marks your current radius.

5. Discover an equipotential

Moving around the same circle preserves r, so V is unchanged even though the direction of E changes.
