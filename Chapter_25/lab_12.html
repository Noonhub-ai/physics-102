Two Connected Conducting Spheres

Two conducting spheres are joined by a thin wire. Charge moves until they reach the same electric potential. Compare how much charge each sphere holds, and discover why the smaller sphere has a stronger surface electric field.

V₁ = V₂   ·   q₁/q₂ = R₁/R₂   ·   E₁/E₂ = R₂/R₁

1. Compare the connected spheres

The sphere sizes change with the radius sliders. Surface dots represent charge schematically, not individual electrons. The wire indicates electrical connection; its length and separation are not to scale.

2. Change the physical system

Model assumption: The spheres are very far apart compared with their radii, and the connecting wire is thin. Mutual influence and the wire's charge are neglected.

4. Predict the outcome

    5. Discover what stays equal

    Explore why equal potential does not mean equal charge or equal surface field.

    Equal potential is a condition of electrostatic equilibrium for conductors connected by a wire. The radii and total charge determine the shared potential in the far-separated-sphere approximation.
