Electric Field Superposition

Explore Example 8 (pages 33–35). Two charges create separate fields at point P. Their vector sum gives the net electric field.

4 · Step-by-step worked solution

Step 1 — Coordinates and distances

Take q₁ at (−a, 0), q₂ at (+b, 0) and P at (0, y).

r₁ = √(a² + y²) = m
r₂ = √(b² + y²) = m
Step 2 — Apply the point-charge field equation

The magnitude of each electric field is k|q|/r², where k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C² and 1 μC = 10⁻⁶ C.

Step 3 — Resolve each field into x and y components

Use the signed source charges to get the correct directions. The unit direction from each source charge toward P is proportional to (a, y) or (−b, y), respectively.

Step 4 — Add the vectors

Superposition: add x components and y components separately, then calculate magnitude and direction.

Step 5 — Special case: symmetric electric dipole

For q₁ = +q at x = −a and q₂ = −q at x = +a, the two vertical components cancel, while both horizontal components point toward +x.

Eᵧ = 0
Eₓ = 2kqa / (a² + y²)³ᐟ²

Here q is the positive magnitude of either charge. This corresponds to the dipole illustration on page 35. In the general case, the vertical components do not necessarily cancel.
